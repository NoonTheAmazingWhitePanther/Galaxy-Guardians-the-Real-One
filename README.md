🌌 What Is Galaxy Guardians?

Galaxy Guardians: The Real One is a browser‑native splatter‑physics sandbox where jelly planets, comets, stars, asteroids, and cosmic nonsense collide, splat, and smear across the screen.

At first glance, it looks like a toy.

Then you realize the toy is secretly teaching you how simulations work – while planets burst like overripe fruit.

Education comes through fun.
And fun comes through maximum, glorious destruction.

One moment you're spawning soft‑body planets that wobble like giant jelly beans.
The next moment you're watching them tear apart, melt into goo, or explode into a thousand sticky blobs because you cranked the sun's heat too high.

Inspired by the spirit of play (the kind where things go BOOM and everyone laughs), Galaxy Guardians treats curiosity and carnage as the main game mechanics.

🎉 Fun First. Education Second. Gore Third. (The Fun Kind of Gore.)

Most educational projects start with education and try to make it fun later.
Galaxy Guardians starts with fun, adds gooey destruction, and sneaks in the learning while you're laughing.

Failure is not just part of the gameplay – it's the best part.
Every explosion is a learning moment covered in jelly.

🎛️ Tune Everything Live – Including the Gore

Galaxy Guardians is designed to be adjusted while it's running.
Crank up the destruction with real‑time sliders:

· Gravity strength – make planets pancake
· Spring stiffness – juicy wobble or instant splat
· Damping – slow‑motion goo
· Planet density – squishy or crunchy
· Solar intensity – melting speed
· Simulation speed – bullet‑time carnage
· Frame skipping
· Physics skipping
· Render interpolation
· Time scaling
· Replay speed – watch that planet die again and again

Turn a stable solar system into a disaster movie.
Turn a disaster into a beautiful, educational gorefest.
Turn a simulation into a game where destruction is the goal.

---

⚡ The Fun Engine (Now With Extra Gore)

The engine is built on a core idea: simulation and rendering don't have to move at the same speed – which means more explosions per second.

· Physics can run independently – so jelly can fly everywhere
· Rendering can run independently – smooth slow‑mo gore
· Simulation states can be cached, stored, replayed, and blended – relive every glorious splat

Future systems may include persistent goo puddles and planet gibs that float forever.

The goal is simple:

Keep the universe moving.
Keep the controls responsive.
Keep the jelly flying.
Keep the fun (and gore) alive.
